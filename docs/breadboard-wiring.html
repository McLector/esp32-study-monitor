Wiring checklist

Phase 2 · Bare ESP32

Gate: Serial prints the correct Manila time within 10 s of boot.

Phase 3 · I2C bus and OLED

Gate: I2C scanner finds 0x3C; "DeskPulse 2.0" is readable.

Phase 4 · Outputs

Gate: LEDs chase 1 → 5; buzzer clicks.

Phase 5 · Inputs

Gate: IR 1 idle / 0 with a hand at 10 cm; PIR 0 at rest; AO spikes on a clap; DHT gives a plausible room reading.

Numbered notes

Match the black badges on the diagram.

  • 1
    GND rails tied The guide puts 3V3 and 5V on opposite rails with GND tied together, so a stray jumper can't put 5 V on a 3.3 V part.
  • 2
    LEDs Long leg (anode, +) goes to the resistor side, short leg to GND. One 220 Ω per LED. Left to right is the progress bar: blue 0–20% → red 80–100%.
  • 3
    IR on 3.3 V, no divider (changed from the guide) This IR module only holds its output HIGH weakly, through about 10 kΩ. The guide's 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider dragged it down to about 1.7 V at GPIO 18. That read as 0 all the time, and the obstacle LED glowed faintly. Powered from 3.3 V, the output can't go above 3.3 V, so it goes straight to GPIO 18. The bench test confirmed the cause: pulling R1 turned the faint glow off. Any free strip works as long as IR OUT and the GPIO 18 jumper share it.
  • 4
    IR module Keep the pots where you tuned them. Mount 10–15 cm from your torso and retest in the demo room, wearing what you'll wear.
  • 5
    OLED Pin order varies: GND-VCC-SCL-SDA or VCC-GND-SCL-SDA. Swapping VCC and GND can kill it. Expect 0x3C (sometimes 0x3D). Mount it facing you.
  • 6
    PIR Time-delay pot fully counter-clockwise. Output is already 3.3 V logic, so no divider. Give it ~60 s after power-up.
  • 7
    LM393 Only AO is used; GPIO 34 is ADC1, which you need with Wi-Fi on. DO stays unwired. The buzzer sits at the other end of the board, about 10 cm away, as the guide asks.
Power (from the guide): laptop port or a 5 V adapter rated 1 A or more, with a data-capable USB cable. A weak port browns out on Wi-Fi bursts: random resets with "Brownout detector was triggered" on Serial.
Watch out (not from the guide): The ESP32 sits off the breadboard in this drawing, so its 15 wires need female-to-male jumpers. Rebuilding from 1.0? IR moved from GPIO 22 to 18 and DHT11 off 21/22, so don't reuse old jumper positions. DHT11, buzzer, IR and PIR modules also differ in pin order between sellers. Wire by the silkscreen label, not by position. Some full-size breadboards split each power rail in the middle; if yours does, bridge the two halves.

GPIOs, rails, resistor values and module notes follow the guide's "Pin map and wiring", "Power rails", "I2C wiring" and "Build order" sections. One deliberate change: the IR module runs on 3.3 V with no divider instead of the guide's "IR voltage divider" (note 3). Hole positions (rows and columns) are a layout choice for this drawing; the guide doesn't fix them, so moving a part is fine as long as each connection stays the same.

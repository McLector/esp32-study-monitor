Overview

DeskPulse 2.0 is a clean rebuild: the same sensors, a new pin map built around an I2C bus, and modular firmware that the four enhancements slot into without rewrites.

Area 1.0 2.0
Local feedback 5 LEDs 5 LEDs + 128x64 OLED status screen
Sit limit Fixed 30 min Blynk sliders for sit limit and break length, saved on the device (25/5 default)
Data Raw counters Daily focus score 0-100, plus one history point per day
Reminders Break alert Break alert, 20-20-20 eye-rest, session summary push
Offline use Details only on Blynk Fully usable offline; the cloud is optional
Reboot All counters lost Today's counters restored from flash
Cloud traffic Every value every 2 s Change-only writes on a slow cadence that fits the free plan

How to use this guide: work the phases in order and do not start one until the previous gate test passes. Keep TEST_MODE on until the final integration run.

Assumptions: Arduino IDE 2.x, Espressif's ESP32 board package, the Blynk library, and your existing Blynk template extended rather than recreated.

Honesty note: the code here is written against the Arduino-ESP32, Adafruit and Blynk library APIs as I understand them. I have not compiled or run it. Treat each snippet as a reference implementation and compile it at the end of its phase.

Decisions

Design decisions and pushback

Seven decisions shape the rebuild. Two of them (the display choice and the Blynk message budget) are corrections to the proposal as written.

Decision 01

Use the 0.96" SSD1306 OLED, not the 16x2 LCD. The proposal leaves this open; I'd close it. The OLED runs natively at 3.3 V and shows 8 lines plus a drawn progress bar. Common 16x2 LCD backpacks (PCF8574) want 5 V and pull SDA/SCL up to 5 V, which is above the ESP32's input rating. You would need a level shifter just to stay in spec.

Decision 02

Cloud traffic must be budgeted. The Blynk Free plan allows 100,000 device messages per month, and every Blynk.virtualWrite() counts as one. Version 1.0 pushed about 7 values every 2 s, roughly 12,600 messages per hour. On a current Free account that empties the month's quota in about 8 hours of use. Version 2.0 sends values only when they change, on slow timers, for a worst case near 500 per hour (budget in the Blynk section). Check the message counter in Blynk.Console: if your older account shows no limit, the budget still costs you nothing.

Decision 03

Only three custom Blynk events. Free templates get 5 events, and community reports say the built-in Online and Offline events count toward that. Plan on exactly three: break_time, eye_rest, session_summary. Each device is also capped at 100 logged events per day.

Decision 04

Weekly history is at the edge of the Free plan. Free keeps 1 week of compressed history. So the firmware writes one "daily focus" value per day to its own datastream; a 7-day chart of that is the weekly view. Anything older than a week is gone unless you upgrade.

Decision 05

The device must work with Wi-Fi off. With an OLED on board, the cloud becomes optional. The firmware connects without blocking, keeps counting while offline, and catches up when the link returns. Settings and today's counters live in flash (NVS), which also fixes 1.0's lost-on-reboot limitation.

Decision 06

Two things cannot be measured, so they are not scored. No sensor here can see where your eyes are, so 20-20-20 is a reminder only. The MPU6050 cannot tell "idle" from "reading a book", so it is optional, off by default, and capped at a small share of the score.

Decision 07

Build the MPU6050 last, and only if time is left. It touches the same I2C bus as the OLED and needs its own tuning session. The other three enhancements do not depend on it.

One small extension to the proposal: the OLED gets two more states besides Studying, Break Time! and Away. "On break" shows a countdown until the break counts, and "Object?" appears when the PIR guard thinks a bag, not a person, is in front of the IR sensor.

Hardware

Pin map and wiring

The I2C bus takes GPIO 21 and 22, the ESP32's default SDA/SCL pins, so the DHT11 and IR sensor move off them. Everything else stays on pins you already know are safe.

Still off limits: GPIO 6-11 (on-board flash) and the strapping pins 0, 2, 5, 12, 15. GPIO 34-39 are input-only. Never use ADC2 pins for analog reads while Wi-Fi is on.

Power rails

Use the two long rails on opposite sides of the breadboard: 5V (VIN) on one side, 3V3 on the other, with GND tied together. Physical separation stops a stray jumper from putting 5 V on a 3.3 V part.

  • 5V rail: PIR only.
  • 3V3 rail: OLED, MPU6050, DHT11, LM393, buzzer, IR obstacle module.
  • Powering from USB: the ESP32's VIN pin carries roughly the USB 5 V (a little less on boards with a protection diode). That is plenty for the PIR.

IR wiring (changed during the build)

This guide originally put the IR module on the 5 V rail, with a 1 kΩ + 2 kΩ divider to bring its output down to 3.3 V for GPIO 18. On the bench that never worked: GPIO 18 read 0 all the time, and the module's obstacle LED glowed faintly with nothing in front of it.

Why: this module doesn't drive its output to 5 V. It only holds it HIGH weakly, through a pull-up of roughly 10 kΩ. A 3 kΩ divider to GND is a much stronger load, so the idle output sagged to about 2.5 V and the divider tap to about 1.7 V. The ESP32 needs about 2.5 V to read a 1. Pulling R1 out confirmed it: the faint glow vanished, and the LED lit only for a hand.

What the build uses instead: run the IR module from 3V3 and wire OUT straight to GPIO 18. On 3.3 V the output can never go above 3.3 V, so no divider is needed:

IR VCC ── 3V3 rail
IR GND ── GND rail
IR OUT ────────────── GPIO 18   (INPUT, no pull-up)

Trade-off: the IR emitter is dimmer on 3.3 V, so the range drops to about 10 cm, which still meets the phase 5 gate. Mount it 8–10 cm from your torso. If you need the full 5 V range, keep it on 5 V with a high-value divider (about 47 kΩ over 100 kΩ). That loads the output so little that the idle tap stays near 3.2 V. Don't use small resistors like 220 Ω–2 kΩ for this.

Before you connect GPIO 18: check by eye that nothing from the IR module is in the 5 V rail. With the module powered and nothing in front of it, its obstacle LED must be fully off.

I2C wiring

OLED and MPU6050 share the same four wires: VCC to 3V3, GND, SDA to 21, SCL to 22. Read the OLED's silkscreen first: some boards are GND-VCC-SCL-SDA, others VCC-GND-SCL-SDA. Swapping VCC and GND can kill the module. Expected addresses: OLED 0x3C (sometimes 0x3D), MPU6050 0x68. They do not collide.

Bench sketch for phase 5

#include <DHT.h>
DHT dht(16, DHT11);

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  pinMode(18, INPUT);   // IR OUT, direct (module on 3V3): no internal pull-up
  pinMode(23, INPUT);   // PIR
  dht.begin();
}

void loop() {
  // Peak-to-peak over 25 ms catches claps a single read misses
  int lo = 4095, hi = 0;
  uint32_t t0 = millis();
  while (millis() - t0 < 25) {
    int v = analogRead(34);
    lo = min(lo, v);
    hi = max(hi, v);
  }
  Serial.printf("IR=%d PIR=%d P2P=%d T=%.1f H=%.0f\n",
                digitalRead(18), digitalRead(23), hi - lo,
                dht.readTemperature(), dht.readHumidity());
  delay(500);
}

The PIR needs about 60 s after power-up before its readings mean anything.

Firmware

Firmware architecture

The firmware splits into logic modules that know nothing about Blynk, plus one cloud layer that does. That split is what lets the device run offline and keeps cloud code from leaking into sensor code.

File layout

DeskPulse/
├── DeskPulse.ino     setup(), loop(), timers, onSignal(); includes Blynk
├── cloud.ino         BLYNK_WRITE handlers, pushes, events (2nd .ino tab)
├── config.h          pins, flags, timing constants
├── secrets.h         Wi-Fi + Blynk credentials (gitignored)
├── state.h           AppState shared by all modules
├── presence.h/.cpp   IR + PIR debounce, object guard
├── sound.h/.cpp      baseline calibration, peak-to-peak, cooldown
├── env.h/.cpp        DHT11
├── session.h/.cpp    state machine, eye-rest, focus score
├── display.h/.cpp    OLED screens
├── buzzer.h/.cpp     non-blocking beep patterns + LED effects
├── storage.h/.cpp    NVS save/restore
└── activity.h/.cpp   MPU6050 (optional, behind USE_MPU)

Why Blynk lives only in .ino tabs: the Arduino IDE joins all .ino tabs into one file before compiling. BlynkSimpleEsp32.h defines the global Blynk object, so including it in two .cpp files should fail with "multiple definition" linker errors. If you see that error, this is the cause.

How logic talks to the cloud: modules raise signals; the .ino decides what each one means (beep, push, log an event).

// session.h
enum class Signal : uint8_t {
  ModeChanged, BreakDue, EyeRest, Distraction, SessionEnded, DayRolled
};
void onSignal(Signal s);   // implemented in DeskPulse.ino

session.h must also declare sessionTick(), tickEyeRest(), computeFocus() and resetToday(). sessionTick() calls the other two before their definitions appear, and C++ will not compile that without declarations.

Timed tasks

loop() runs only Blynk.run(), timer.run(), the buzzer sequencer and the mic sampler. No delay() anywhere in the main path.

Every Task Does
20 ms taskActivity MPU6050 sampling (optional)
50 ms taskSound Reads the running peak-to-peak, distraction check
200 ms taskPresence IR + PIR debounce (5 reads = 1 s), object guard
250 ms taskTick Runs the 1 Hz state machine, with catch-up
500 ms taskDisplay Redraws the OLED
2.5 s taskEnvironment DHT11 read
10 s taskConnectivity Wi-Fi and Blynk health
30 s taskCloud Throttled pushes (Blynk section)
30 s taskRollover Midnight reset via NTP
60 s taskPersist Saves today's counters to NVS if they changed

Catch-up tick. A Blynk reconnect attempt can stall loop() for a few seconds. A timer that just fires "once per second" would then lose seconds. Count elapsed time instead:

void taskTick() {
  static uint32_t last = millis();
  while (millis() - last >= 1000) {   // runs extra ticks after a stall
    last += 1000;
    sessionTick();
  }
}

Sound sampling: two fixes over 1.0

Mute the mic while the buzzer sounds. The buzzer sits centimetres from the LM393. During Break due it double-beeps every 5 s, and each beep would log as a distraction; eye-rest chirps would too. Ignore sound while the buzzer is on and for 300 ms after. The build log doesn't say whether 1.0 handled this, so check your old code.

Stop busy-waiting. 1.0's taskSound blocked the loop for 25 ms out of every 50 ms. Add an OLED redraw (about 25 ms) and the MPU's 20 ms task, and timing gets jittery. Instead, sample once per loop() pass and keep a running min/max:

// sound.cpp
static uint16_t lo = 4095, hi = 0;

void soundSample() {                       // every loop() pass
  const uint16_t v = analogRead(PIN_SOUND_AO);
  if (v < lo) lo = v;
  if (v > hi) hi = v;
}

void taskSound() {                         // every 50 ms
  const uint16_t p2p = hi > lo ? hi - lo : 0;
  lo = 4095; hi = 0;
  if (buzzerActiveWithin(300)) return;     // our own beep is not a distraction
  if (p2p > app.live.noisePeak) app.live.noisePeak = p2p;   // V4 push resets it
  checkDistraction(p2p);                   // baseline + margin, 3 s cooldown, present only
}

Trade-off: a sound shorter than a blocking call (an OLED redraw, a Blynk reconnect) can still slip through. It is a smaller gap than 1.0 had.

setup() and loop()

AppState app;
BlynkTimer timer;

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  loadSettings(app.cfg);
  loadDaily(app.day);                  // checked against the date once NTP syncs
  initHardware();                      // pins, Wire.begin(PIN_SDA, PIN_SCL), OLED, DHT
  calibrateSound();                    // 2 s ambient baseline, as in 1.0
  WiFi.mode(WIFI_STA);
  WiFi.begin(WIFI_SSID, WIFI_PASS);
  configTime(8 * 3600, 0, "pool.ntp.org", "time.google.com");  // Manila, no DST
  Blynk.config(BLYNK_AUTH_TOKEN);      // non-blocking; do not use Blynk.begin()
  registerTimers();
}

void loop() {
  if (WiFi.status() == WL_CONNECTED) Blynk.run();
  timer.run();
  buzzerUpdate();
  soundSample();                       // non-blocking mic sampling (see above)
}

Blynk.begin() blocks until it connects, which would freeze the whole device without Wi-Fi. That is the reason for Blynk.config().

State machine

Four modes drive everything the user sees. The key rule: a break only counts once you have stayed away for the full break length.

Leaving always starts On break; only a full break length away counts the break and resets the sit and eye-rest timers.

  • Present means IR active for 5 straight reads (1 s). Absent means 5 straight inactive reads.
  • Object guard: IR active but no PIR motion for OBJECT_GUARD_S is treated as absent, and the OLED shows "Object?". The guard stays in real seconds even in TEST_MODE: scaled to 5 s, simply sitting still until the PIR drops would flag you as a bag.
  • Studying: sit timer, eye-rest timer and study minutes all count up each second.
  • Break due starts when the sit timer reaches the limit. Alert pattern every 5 s, plus one break_time event.
  • Skipped break: still seated BREAK_GRACE_MIN after the alert started. Counted once per cycle.
  • Leaving early (before the break length is up) and coming back returns you to your previous mode with the sit timer kept. A trip to the door is not a break.
  • Session end: away for the larger of SESSION_END_MIN and break length + 5 minutes.

Init

#include <Wire.h>
#include <Adafruit_GFX.h>
#include <Adafruit_SSD1306.h>

Adafruit_SSD1306 oled(128, 64, &Wire, -1);   // -1: no reset pin

bool displayInit() {
  Wire.begin(PIN_SDA, PIN_SCL);
  Wire.setClock(400000);                       // 400 kHz: a full 1 KB frame still takes about 25 ms
  if (!oled.begin(SSD1306_SWITCHCAPVCC, 0x3C)) // try 0x3D if this fails
    return false;
  oled.setTextColor(SSD1306_WHITE);
  oled.clearDisplay();
  oled.display();
  return true;
}

If displayInit() returns false, keep running without the screen and print a warning to Serial. A dead display must not stop the device.

Render

static void mmss(char* out, size_t n, uint32_t sec) {
  snprintf(out, n, "%02lu:%02lu", (unsigned long)(sec / 60), (unsigned long)(sec % 60));
}

void displayRender(const AppState& a, bool wifiUp, bool cloudUp) {
  const Live& L = a.live;
  const uint32_t limit = a.cfg.sitLimitMin * MIN_S;
  const uint32_t brk   = a.cfg.breakMin * MIN_S;
  char t1[8], t2[8];

  oled.clearDisplay();

  if (L.eyeRestLeft > 0) {                         // eye-rest overlay
    oled.setTextSize(2); oled.setCursor(0, 0);  oled.print("EYE REST");
    oled.setTextSize(1); oled.setCursor(0, 22); oled.print("Look 20 ft (6 m) away");
    oled.setTextSize(3); oled.setCursor(46, 38); oled.printf("%2u", L.eyeRestLeft);
    oled.display();
    return;
  }

  oled.setTextSize(2); oled.setCursor(0, 0);
  switch (L.mode) {
    case Mode::Studying: oled.print("STUDYING");   break;
    case Mode::BreakDue: oled.print("BREAK TIME"); break;
    case Mode::OnBreak:  oled.print("ON BREAK");   break;
    case Mode::Away:     oled.print(L.objectSuspected ? "OBJECT?" : "AWAY"); break;
  }

  oled.setTextSize(1); oled.setCursor(0, 20);
  if (L.mode == Mode::OnBreak) {
    mmss(t1, sizeof t1, L.awaySec < brk ? brk - L.awaySec : 0);
    oled.printf("Break counts in %s", t1);
  } else {
    mmss(t1, sizeof t1, L.sitSec);
    mmss(t2, sizeof t2, L.sitSec < limit ? limit - L.sitSec : 0);
    oled.printf("Sit %s  Left %s", t1, t2);
  }

  uint32_t pct = limit ? L.sitSec * 100 / limit : 0;
  if (pct > 100) pct = 100;
  oled.drawRect(0, 31, 128, 7, SSD1306_WHITE);
  oled.fillRect(1, 32, (126 * pct) / 100, 5, SSD1306_WHITE);

  oled.setCursor(0, 42);
  if (isnan(L.tempC)) oled.print("--.-C --%RH");
  else oled.printf("%.1fC %.0f%%RH", L.tempC, L.humidity);
  oled.setCursor(98, 42);
  oled.printf("D:%u", a.day.distractions);

  oled.setCursor(0, 54);

2b. State machine

This implements the diagram in the architecture section. It runs once per (catch-up) second.

// session.cpp
static uint32_t sitLimitSec() { return app.cfg.sitLimitMin * MIN_S; }
static uint32_t breakSec()    { return app.cfg.breakMin * MIN_S; }
static uint32_t sessionEndSec() {
  uint32_t m = app.cfg.breakMin + 5;
  if (m < SESSION_END_MIN) m = SESSION_END_MIN;
  return m * MIN_S;
}

static void setMode(Mode m) {
  if (app.live.mode == m) return;
  app.live.mode = m;
  onSignal(Signal::ModeChanged);
}

static void completeBreak() {
  Live& L = app.live;
  if (L.sitSec >= sitLimitSec() && L.dueSec < BREAK_GRACE_MIN * MIN_S)
    app.day.breaksOnTime++;
  app.day.breaksTaken++;
  app.ses.breaks++;
  L.sitSec = L.dueSec = L.eyeSec = 0;
  L.skipCounted = false;
  setMode(Mode::Away);
}

void sessionTick() {
  Live& L = app.live;
  if (L.present) {
    L.awaySec = 0;
    if (!app.ses.active) { app.ses = Session{}; app.ses.active = true; }
    if (L.mode == Mode::Away || L.mode == Mode::OnBreak)       // back at the desk
      setMode(L.sitSec >= sitLimitSec() ? Mode::BreakDue : Mode::Studying);

    L.sitSec++; app.day.studySec++; app.ses.studySec++;
    tickEyeRest();                                             // Enhancement 3

    if (L.mode == Mode::Studying && L.sitSec >= sitLimitSec()) {
      setMode(Mode::BreakDue);
      onSignal(Signal::BreakDue);                              // once per cycle
    }
    if (L.mode == Mode::BreakDue) {
      L.dueSec++;
      if (!L.skipCounted && L.dueSec >= BREAK_GRACE_MIN * MIN_S) {
        app.day.breaksSkipped++;
        L.skipCounted = true;
      }
    }
  } else {
    L.awaySec++;
    L.eyeRestLeft = 0;                                         // left the desk: overlay off
    if (L.mode == Mode::Studying || L.mode == Mode::BreakDue) setMode(Mode::OnBreak);
    if (L.mode == Mode::OnBreak && L.awaySec >= breakSec()) completeBreak();
    if (app.ses.active && L.awaySec >= sessionEndSec()) {
      app.ses.active = false;
      onSignal(Signal::SessionEnded);                          // Enhancement 4
    }
  }
  L.focus = computeFocus(app.day);
}

The buzzer and LED blink follow mode == BreakDue, so the alert resumes on its own if you come back early.

Consider backing off the alarm. As written, the buzzer double-beeps every 5 s for as long as you ignore it; an hour on a call is 720 beeps. A reasonable rule: every 5 s during the grace window, then every 30 s once the skip has been counted. The LEDs keep blinking either way.

2c. Focus score

The score starts at 100 and loses points for skipped breaks and for the distraction rate. It stays hidden (--) until you have 10 minutes of study time.

F = max(0, 100 − min(45, 15S) − min(40, 4R) − Pidle)
R = 60D / max(T, 30)

T = study minutes today, D = distractions today, S = breaks skipped (not taken within the 5-minute grace), R = distractions per hour. Pidle = min(15, 15 × idle share of study time), and is 0 unless the MPU6050 is fitted.

Why it is built this way:

  • Rate, not count: 6 distractions over 4 hours is better focus than 6 in 30 minutes. This is where study minutes enter the score.
  • 30-minute floor on T: without it, one clap in the first 10 minutes would read as 6 per hour.
  • Caps: no single factor can drive the score to 0 alone.
  • Breaks: "on time" means taken within the grace window. A late break counts as skipped.
Study T Distractions D Skipped S Rate R Score
8 min 1 0 - -- (under 10 min)
20 min 4 0 8.0 / h 68
50 min 2 0 2.4 / h 90
120 min 6 1 3.0 / h 73
180 min 30 3 10.0 / h 15

Use these rows as test cases in phase 9: feed the counters by hand over Serial and compare.

// DeskPulse.ino — test hook; call from loop(), does nothing unless TEST_MODE
// Type into Serial Monitor:  t=120 d=6 s=1   → sets today's counters, prints the score
void serialTestHook() {
  if (!TEST_MODE || !Serial.available()) return;
  String line = Serial.readStringUntil('\n');
  int t, d, s;
  if (sscanf(line.c_str(), "t=%d d=%d s=%d", &t, &d, &s) == 3) {
    app.day.studySec = t * MIN_S;
    app.day.distractions = d;
    app.day.breaksSkipped = s;
    Serial.printf("focus=%d\n", computeFocus(app.day));
  }
}

int8_t computeFocus(const Daily& d) {
  const float studyMin = d.studySec / (float)MIN_S;
  if (studyMin < 10) return -1;                               // not enough data
  const float perHour = d.distractions * 60.0f / (studyMin < 30 ? 30.0f : studyMin);
  float penalty = fminf(45.0f, 15.0f * d.breaksSkipped)
                + fminf(40.0f, 4.0f * perHour);
  if (USE_MPU && d.studySec > 0)
    penalty += fminf(15.0f, 15.0f * d.idleSec / (float)d.studySec);
  const long score = lroundf(100.0f - penalty);
  return (int8_t)(score < 0 ? 0 : score);
}

Be upfront about this in the defense: the weights (15, 4, 45, 40) are design choices, not a validated productivity metric. They are constants in one function, so tuning them is a one-line change.

2d. Daily rollover and history

At local midnight the final score goes to V15 (Daily Focus), then today's counters reset. A 7-day chart on V15 is the weekly view.

void taskRollover() {
  struct tm now;
  if (!getLocalTime(&now, 0)) return;   // 0 ms wait: the default waits up to 5 s
  const uint32_t ymd = (now.tm_year + 1900) * 10000 + (now.tm_mon + 1) * 100 + now.tm_mday;
  if (app.day.ymd == 0) { app.day.ymd = ymd; return; }      // fresh counters, first sync
  if (ymd == app.day.ymd) return;                           // same day (also after a reboot)
  onSignal(Signal::DayRolled);                              // writes the final score to V15
  app.day = Daily{};
  app.day.ymd = ymd;
}

This also covers a reboot: restored counters from today are kept, and counters from an earlier day are closed out on the first NTP sync. One quirk: if the device was off at midnight, yesterday's score is stamped with today's time in Blynk.

No NTP, no date. The ESP32 has no battery-backed clock. If it boots without Wi-Fi and stays offline past midnight, two days of counters merge into one. That is acceptable for a desk demo; a DS3231 RTC module on the same I2C bus would fix it if a panel asks.

Enhancement 3: 20-20-20 eye-rest reminder

After every 20 minutes at the desk, the device chirps three times, sweeps the LEDs once, shows a 20-second countdown on the OLED and logs an eye_rest event. It is skipped when a break is due within 3 minutes, because the break rests your eyes anyway.

Rules

void tickEyeRest() {

Enhancement 04

Enhancement 4: session summary notification

When you have been away for the session-end time (10 minutes, or break length + 5 if longer), the device sends one session_summary event, for example: "You studied 52 min, took 2 breaks, had 4 distractions. Focus today: 81."

Rules

  • A session starts the first time you sit down after the previous one ended.
  • Sessions shorter than 5 study minutes send nothing. Sitting down to grab a pen is not a session.
  • The final departure is not a break. It does count as one in the state machine, because you were away for the full break length before the session ended. Subtract it in the summary.
  • If Blynk is offline, the summary waits and is sent on reconnect. Only the latest one is kept.

Stretch goal

Optional: MPU6050 typing-activity detection

Do a 30-minute feasibility test before writing any integration code. If typing does not clearly stand out from rest on your actual desk, drop the feature and report that as a finding.

Wiring and mounting

  • GY-521 VCC to 3V3, GND, SDA to 21, SCL to 22. Leave AD0 unconnected (address 0x68).
  • Mount the module flat on the desk surface near the keyboard, taped or hot-glued down. Not on the breadboard: touching jumper wires would read as typing.
  • Keep the I2C lead short. If you see read errors on a longer run, drop the bus to 100 kHz.

Feasibility test

  1. Flash a sketch that prints the 1-second vibration energy (code below) to the Serial Plotter.
  2. Type for 30 s, then sit still with hands off for 30 s. Repeat three times.
  3. Go if every typing stretch sits clearly above the resting band. No-go if they overlap.

Test with the keyboard you will demo on. A laptop keyboard may pass less vibration into the desk than an external one; I don't know how much on your setup.

Detection

#include <Adafruit_MPU6050.h>
Adafruit_MPU6050 mpu;
float activityThreshold = 0;          // set by a 5 s calibration at boot, hands off

bool activityInit() {
  if (!mpu.begin(0x68, &Wire)) return false;
  mpu.setAccelerometerRange(MPU6050_RANGE_2_G);   // most sensitive range
  mpu.setFilterBandwidth(MPU6050_BAND_94_HZ);     // keeps short taps; tune
  return true;
}

// every 20 ms → 50 samples per 1 s window
void taskActivity() {
  static float avg = 0, energy = 0;
  static uint8_t n = 0;
  sensors_event_t a, g, temp;
  mpu.getEvent(&a, &g, &temp);
  const float mag = sqrtf(a.acceleration.x * a.acceleration.x +
                          a.acceleration.y * a.acceleration.y +
                          a.acceleration.z * a.acceleration.z);
  if (avg == 0) avg = mag;
  avg += 0.02f * (mag - avg);           // slow average ≈ gravity
  energy += fabsf(mag - avg);           // vibration on top of it
  if (++n >= 50) {
    windowResult(energy / n > activityThreshold);
    energy = 0;
    n = 0;
  }
}
  • Calibration: during the 5 s, record each window's energy; start with threshold = mean + 4 standard deviations, then tune from the plot.
  • Active = at least 3 of the last 10 windows above threshold.
  • Idle = present but not active for 2 minutes straight. Each idle second adds to app.day.idleSec.

How it enters the score

Idle is not the same as unproductive: reading, handwriting notes and watching a lecture all look idle. So the idle penalty is capped at 15 points and is applied only when USE_MPU is true. My recommendation is to show "Active minutes" (V16) on the dashboard and keep it out of the score unless your feasibility test was convincing.

Logistics

Project logistics

These are not firmware problems, but each one has sunk projects like this before.

  • Freeze 1.0 first. Commit study_monitor.ino, BUILD_GUIDE.md and SESSION_HANDOFF.md to a git repo and tag it v1.0 before you change anything. Then make one commit per passed gate, so a broken phase can be diffed against the last green one.
  • Pin library versions. Once phase 1 compiles, record the ESP32 core, Blynk, Adafruit SSD1306/GFX, DHT and MPU6050 versions in a README. Don't update mid-project. I have not verified the current Blynk library against ESP32 core 3.x; phase 1's compile gate is where you find out.
  • Blynk Free allows 1 user. Only one group member can log into the Console. Decide who owns it, and share secrets.h privately, never in the repo.
  • Split by module. If the work is shared across the group, AppState and Signal are the seams. For example: wiring and bench tests (phases 2-5), core and state machine (6, 8), OLED and eye-rest (7, 10), Blynk, score and summary (9, 11). Merge often; the .ino tabs are the conflict hotspot.
  • Plan the demo now. A panel won't wait 25 minutes. Flash a TEST_MODE build for the live demo and label which build is on the board. Bring evidence from real-time use: V10 and V15 charts and a real summary push, as screenshots.
  • Physical layout. The IR sensor must sit 10-15 cm from your torso, the OLED must face you, and the microphone should be as far from the buzzer as the breadboard allows. Decide where the board sits on the desk before final wiring; use longer jumpers for the IR head rather than for I2C. Then retest the IR in defense conditions: these modules reflect less off black or dark fabric and can be blinded by sunlight or strong lamps. Check the 10-15 cm range wearing what you will wear, in the room you will demo in, and bring a small screwdriver for the pots.
  • Power. Use a laptop port or a 5 V adapter rated 1 A or more, with a data-capable cable. Wi-Fi transmit bursts can brown out a weak port; the sign is random resets with "Brownout detector was triggered" on Serial.
  • Cut line. All four enhancements are promised; only the MPU6050 is "if time permits". If time runs short, cut in this order: the MPU6050, the V4 noise chart, mobile dashboard polish. Do not cut a promised enhancement.
Defense

Known limitations and defense notes

2.0 fixes one of 1.0's limitations outright (counters now survive reboots) and keeps the rest. Say these plainly in the defense; a panel trusts a system whose limits you can name.

Limitation Status in 2.0 What to say
IR range 10-15 cm Unchanged Mount at the desk edge, aimed at the torso
No identity Unchanged Anyone seated accumulates study time
PIR sees the whole room Unchanged The object guard reduces false study time, not to zero. It also cuts the other way: sitting very still for 5 minutes (deep reading) can be read as an object
Lost counters on reboot Fixed Today's counters and settings are saved in flash
Sound is amplitude only Unchanged A sneeze, a door and a voice are the same event
DHT11 accuracy Unchanged Fine for "is the room uncomfortable", not for precision
Noise baseline set once at boot Unchanged Reboot to recalibrate. Cheap fix if asked: re-learn it slowly while AWAY
Eye-rest compliance New, by design Nothing can verify where you looked, so it is a reminder and is not scored
Focus score validity New, by design A transparent heuristic with chosen weights, not a validated metric
Idle vs. reading (MPU) New, by design Accelerometer idle is not unproductive; hence the 15-point cap
Blynk Free plan New constraint 100,000 messages a month, 3 usable custom events, 1 week of history
OLED burn-in New, mitigated Dims when away, switches off after the session ends

Expect a privacy question. The device records no audio and no images. The LM393 reports a loudness level, not sound; only counts, levels and the summary text leave the device, and only to your own Blynk account.
